## Voiceover script with timestamps from the recording

Read at a calm pace. Each block is timed to what is on screen.

[0:00 to 0:28, landing page]
This is Blend, at blend dot tanmaytiwari dot me. Blend is a private comparison tool for two people. Each person connects their own YouTube account, and Blend shows what their subscriptions, liked videos, and playlists have in common. Nothing is posted, liked, edited, or deleted on YouTube. The homepage says this before anyone signs in, and the user must agree to the privacy policy and terms before Continue with Google works.

[0:28 to 0:50, account chooser and warning]
Sign-in uses Google OAuth. Blend never sees a password. The first person picks their Google account. Because the app is not yet verified, Google shows its warning, and the user continues through Advanced.

[0:50 to 1:00, basic sign-in screen]
Google first confirms the basic sign-in: name, profile picture, and email address. Blend uses these only to show whose account is connected.

[1:00 to 1:30, consent screen with the YouTube scope]
This is the consent screen. Blend requests one YouTube scope: youtube dot readonly, shown as View your YouTube account. The access details show what it covers: viewing videos, playlists, and YouTube activity. There is no write scope. Blend cannot change anything on the channel. The user ticks the scope and continues.

[1:30 to 2:00, gathering screen]
This is the first connection, so Blend is calling the YouTube Data API right now with the read-only token. It reads the subscriptions list, the liked videos, and the user's playlists. Nothing is cached yet. This takes a moment for a large account.

[2:00 to 2:50, dashboard]
The result is a personal snapshot: the channels followed, the videos kept, the music found among them, and the playlists. Every item links back to YouTube. The profile title and the interest labels are generated by Blend from these counts. They are not YouTube metrics. This snapshot stays private until the user invites someone.

[2:50 to 3:12, invite room]
The user creates a private room. Blend generates a link that expires after two hours and closes after one person joins. The host copies the link and sends it to a friend.

[3:12 to 3:30, guest invitation]
The second person opens the link. Before connecting, they see exactly what Blend reads, what the room shows to both people, and what Blend never changes. They agree to share the resulting comparison with the person who invited them.

[3:30 to 4:25, guest consent]
The second person signs in with their own Google account. They go through the same steps: the account chooser, the unverified-app warning, the basic sign-in confirmation, and the same single read-only YouTube scope. They tick the scope and continue, and Blend fetches their snapshot from the API in the same way.

[4:25 to 4:50, reveal chapter one, Meet]
When both people have authorised, Blend builds the reveal. The first chapter introduces each person's account with their counts and Blend-generated profile, so both people see what was read before any comparison.

[4:50 to 5:20, Signal and Shared]
Next is a similarity score. It is calculated by Blend from exact overlap and is not a YouTube or Google metric. Then the exact matches: channels and saved videos that appear on both sides.

[5:20 to 5:55, Swap and Shelves]
Swap lists real items from the other person's snapshot that did not match. They are not algorithmic predictions. Shelves shows both snapshots side by side, with every item linking back to YouTube.

[5:55 to 6:10, Shape]
Shape groups interest labels into shared, mostly yours, and mostly theirs, with per-category overlap percentages. The labels are generated by Blend from YouTube video metadata.

[6:10 to 6:20, Keep]
The final chapter summarises the result. Both people can share this summary or start another Blend.

[6:20 to 6:39, Settings]
Users stay in control. From Settings they can sign out, disconnect Google, which revokes the token and ends every session, or delete all account data, which permanently removes their snapshot, sessions, and comparisons. That is the complete use of the YouTube read-only scope in Blend.

## Scope justification text (paste into the console)

Blend is a private two-person comparison of YouTube taste. The youtube.readonly scope is required to read the signed-in user's own subscriptions (subscriptions.list), liked videos (videos.list with myRating, and playlistItems.list on the Liked videos playlist), and playlists (playlists.list and playlistItems.list). Blend uses this data only to build a personal snapshot and compute exact overlap with one other consenting user. No narrower scope exposes subscriptions and playlists. Blend never writes to YouTube: it does not like, subscribe, comment, upload, or edit anything. Data is stored only as a snapshot per user, is shown only to the two participants of a room, and can be deleted by the user at any time from Settings, which also revokes the token. See the demo video for the full flow.
